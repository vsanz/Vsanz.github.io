About

I am a theoretical physicist working on particle physics, effective field theories and the statistical interpretation of collider data, and on the use of machine learning to understand physical systems. My research connects microscopic dynamics and symmetries with observables and effective descriptions, and increasingly asks the same questions of complex systems outside physics.

After a PhD at the Universitat de València (2002), I held research positions at MIT, Harvard, Durham and Boston University, a Juan de la Cierva fellowship in Granada, and faculty positions at York University in Toronto and the University of Sussex, where I rose from Lecturer to Full Professor and co-founded the Data Intensive Science Centre. I was also an Associate at CERN in 2012 and at the Alan Turing Institute in London in 2019. I returned to Valencia in 2019 as a Beatriz Galindo Distinguished Senior Researcher and was appointed Full Professor in 2023.

I have published around 200 papers, cited more than 17,000 times (h-index 55, Google Scholar), and supervised or co-supervised twelve doctoral theses. My former students now work as university faculty, postdoctoral researchers and industrial R&D specialists.

Research

Physics beyond the Standard Model

How do we look for new physics when no new particle shows up directly? Much of my work uses effective field theories to turn precision measurements at the LHC into model-independent statements about heavier physics, and maps those statements back to concrete models. This includes global fits to Higgs, top, diboson and electroweak data, the phenomenology of axion-like particles, and reinterpreting published LHC measurements to test new theories.

SMEFT and HEFT global analyses, axion-like particles, composite Higgs and extra dimensions, dark matter and its mediators, gravitational waves from dark sectors, cosmology and inflation.

Learning from physical data

One line of my research studies how neural networks learn physical representations: whether they find the symmetries of a dataset, what structure their latent spaces encode, and how their outputs can be turned into statements with proper statistical meaning. In collider physics this has led to unbinned multivariate observables for EFT fits, anomaly detection for unexpected signals, and symbolic regression that recovers interpretable formulas from data.

Symmetry discovery, interpretable machine learning, symbolic regression, anomaly detection, generative models, from model outputs to statistical criteria.

My current agenda brings both programmes together around emergence. Starting with coupled oscillators, where the order parameter is known, and moving towards reaction–diffusion systems, the aim is to understand when effective descriptions can be learned from data, how well they generalise, and where they break down. Related work treats axion misalignment as a synchronisation phenomenon.

Across disciplines

The methods developed for particle physics, especially for modelling uncertainty and spotting rare events, carry over to other fields. With ecologists, engineers, clinicians and social scientists, I have worked on predicting species coexistence in plant communities, forecasting traffic and air pollution in Valencia, predicting geomagnetic storms, improving Compton-camera medical imaging, and analysing political discourse.

Some of this work now reaches society through technology transfer. I drove the licensing agreement that created INFERA Labs, the first AI spin-off of the Universitat de València, where I am co-founder and Chief Scientific Advisor, and I lead STEP, a project on AI for quantum instrumentation and magnetometry.

Ecology and ecological restoration, urban mobility and air quality, space weather, medical imaging, seismology, computational social science, generative AI and music.

Teaching and supervision

I teach quantum physics and elementary particle physics at the Universitat de València, at undergraduate and master’s level, and previously taught across the Physics and Astronomy programmes at Sussex and York. I have lectured at doctoral schools including TASI, the CERN–JINR European School, DESY, the Perimeter Institute and, in 2025, graduate lectures on machine learning for physics at the Interdisciplinary Center for Scientific Computing in Heidelberg.

At the Universitat de València I also train fellow lecturers in generative AI and AI agents for teaching and research. I have supervised or co-supervised twelve PhD theses and more than thirty master’s and undergraduate projects, on topics from collider phenomenology and gravity to machine learning for dark-matter detection.

In outreach, I co-wrote the illustrated children’s book Discovering Energy, published in English, Spanish and Catalan.
